Does the 14-day closed test reset when testers drop out?

Updated November 2026

Short answer: one tester leaving doesn't reset anything by itself, but falling below 12 opted-in testers does break your progress. Google counts testers who have been opted in continuously, so the people who leave, and the people who replace them, change the math. This guide explains how the count works according to Google's Help Center, what to do mid-test, and why 15 is the number to aim for.

What Google actually counts

Google's requirement for personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 is a closed test with at least 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days. Three details in Google's wording matter here:

  • The 14 days must be consecutive. There is no pausing and resuming.
  • Testers who opt in, test for fewer than 14 days and then opt out don't count toward the requirement.
  • At least 12 testers must be opted in when you apply for production access.

Think of it per tester: each person has their own clock that starts when they opt in. You qualify when at least 12 people have each been opted in for 14 days in a row, and are still opted in.

Common scenarios

  • You had 15, one leaves on day 8. The other 14 keep counting. On day 14 you still have 14 qualifying testers. No problem.
  • You had exactly 12, one leaves on day 8. Now only 11 people can reach 14 days. You need a replacement, and that person's 14 days start on the day they opt in. Your earliest apply date moves back about a week.
  • A tester opts out and back in. Google says opting out and back in restarts that tester's consecutive count.
  • A tester stays opted in but stops opening the app. They still count toward 12 for the opt-in rule, but they hurt you on engagement, which Google asks about in the production form.
  • A tester uninstalls the app but doesn't opt out. They are probably still opted in, but they aren't testing. Treat them like the previous case.

Why ask for 15 testers, not 12

Dropouts are normal. People change phones, forget, run out of storage or lose interest. With exactly 12, any single dropout before day 14 pushes your apply date back. With 15, you can lose three people and still apply on time.

It also helps engagement. Google looks at how testers used the app. If three of fifteen are quiet, the other twelve still show steady use. If three of twelve are quiet, a quarter of your test looks inactive.

What to do mid-test

  1. Check the opted-in tester count in Play Console every two or three days.
  2. If you see a drop, add replacements at once. Every day you wait is a day added to their 14.
  3. Message quiet testers with one concrete task ("try the new search screen in build 1.0.3") rather than a general reminder.
  4. Ship an update during the test. A fresh build gives testers a reason to open the app again.
  5. Don't apply until you have at least 12 testers who have each passed 14 consecutive days and are still opted in. A margin of two or three is safer.

Things that don't reset your progress

  • Uploading new builds to the same closed track. Testers stay opted in and get the update.
  • Adding more testers. New people start their own 14 days; existing testers keep theirs.
  • Editing the store listing or release notes.

Things to avoid: removing the email list or Google Group from the track, or deleting the track. Testers may lose access, and it's unclear how Google then treats their opt-in, so don't risk it.

Frequently asked questions

If one tester leaves, does my 14 days start over?

Not for the others. Each tester's clock is separate. You only lose time if you fall below 12 testers who can complete 14 consecutive days.

Does a replacement tester start at day 1?

Yes. Their 14 consecutive days start when they opt in.

What if a tester opts out by mistake and rejoins?

Google says opting out and back in restarts their consecutive count.

Do inactive testers count?

If they stay opted in, they count toward the 12. But low engagement is a common reason Google asks for more testing.

How many spare testers should I have?

Aim for 15 in total, three above Google's minimum.
