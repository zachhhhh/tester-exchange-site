Your options and their trade-offs

Every option below can get you to 12 names on a list. The real question is whether those 12 people will still be opening your app on day 10, because Google looks at how the app was used, not just who opted in.

  • Friends and family: free and trustworthy, but few people have 12 friends with Android phones who will open an unfinished app every day for two weeks. Expect a lot of reminding.
  • Developer communities and test-for-test swaps: free, and other developers understand the rule. The catch is coordination: you test their apps in return, and informal swaps often fade after a few days with nobody keeping track.
  • Paid tester services: fast, typically about US$10–35 per app. The risk is testers who opt in but never really open the app, which is exactly what Google flags. Ask how daily use is checked before you pay.
  • Cheap freelance gigs: the lowest price, the highest risk. Fake or inactive accounts and many accounts on one shared device are common, and Google checks engagement, so these testers may not count at all.

Tester Exchange: a free swap that keeps everyone honest

Tester Exchange turns the test-for-test swap into something you can rely on. You can use either path, or both at once.

  • Bring your own testers: share your app's link with friends and family. They install the free Tester Exchange app, which checks each of their 14 days for you, so you don't have to chase anyone.
  • Free swap circle: developers test each other's apps for 14 days. A circle starts at 6 developers, or on Monday with at least 3, and friends or credits fill the rest of your 12.
  • Testers from the pool: earn credits by testing other members' apps, then spend them on testers for yours. You can start right away instead of waiting for a circle to fill.
  • Verified daily use: the Tester Exchange Android app checks on each tester's real phone that your app was opened every day, so you know who is actually testing.

How to choose

  • You have 12+ reliable Android friends: use them, give them a daily task, and keep reminding.
  • You have time but no budget: join a swap. A structured one with tracking beats a loose chat thread.
  • You consider paying: only pay for testers whose daily use is checked, and never for installs alone.
  • Whatever you pick: recruit 14–15 testers, not exactly 12, so a few dropouts don't stop the clock.

Checklist: what makes testers count

  • Real people: one person per account, not spare accounts you created yourself.
  • Their own phones: each tester uses their own Android device, not one phone shared by many accounts.
  • Regular use for the full 14 days: testers open the app regularly from day 1 to day 14, not just once after installing.
  • Feedback: testers tell you what broke or confused them, and you ship fixes during the test.

None of this guarantees production access. Google makes the final decision, but a test that ticks every box gives you the strongest case.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really get 12 testers for free?

Yes. Friends, developer communities and swap circles cost nothing but time. On Tester Exchange the swap circle is free, and pool testers are paid with credits you earn by testing.

Are paid tester services worth it?

Only if they can show that testers actually open your app every day. Testers who opt in and never use the app are the most common reason a closed test doesn't pass.

Do testers from swaps count for Google?

They count like any other tester if they are real people using their own phones and opening your app regularly for the full 14 days.

How much time does a swap circle take?

You open each app in your circle for a short while every day for 14 days. It's a small daily habit, and it's what gets your own app 12 active testers.

Does a good closed test guarantee production access?

No. Google decides. Real, active testers and honest answers in the production questionnaire give you the best chance.
