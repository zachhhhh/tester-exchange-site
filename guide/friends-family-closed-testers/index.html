Using friends and family as your 12 Google Play testers

Updated October 2026

Google Play's closed-testing rule for new personal developer accounts asks for at least 12 testers who stay opted in for 14 days in a row. Friends and family are allowed, and they are the most trustworthy testers you will find. The hard part is not asking them; it's getting every one of them to keep opening an unfinished app for two weeks. Here is how to make that work, and what to do when you only have five friends with Android phones.

Can friends and family be closed testers?

Yes. Google doesn't say who your testers must be. What it reviews is whether real people opted in, installed the app from the closed track and used it during the test. A friend on their own Android phone counts exactly like a stranger.

  • Each tester needs their own Google account on the phone they use, opted in through your Google Group or email list.
  • Their own device: five accounts on one phone is the pattern that looks fake. One person, one phone.
  • Installed from Google Play: testers must install from the opt-in link, not from an APK you send them.
  • Opted in for all 14 days: someone who leaves the test on day 9 no longer counts toward the 12.

How to invite them in a way that works

  • Ask for 14–15 people, not 12. Someone always loses their phone, changes accounts or forgets.
  • Use a Google Group. Friends join with one click, and you never paste emails into Play Console again.
  • Send one message with every step: join the group, open the opt-in link, install from Google Play, open the app once a day.
  • Check for iPhones first. Many friends don't have an Android phone; ask before you count them.

Keeping friends active for 14 days

Friends mean well and still forget by day four. Give them a reason and a reminder to open the app every day.

  • A tiny daily task: "tap one thing and tell me if it was confusing" beats "please test my app".
  • Automatic reminders: the Tester Exchange Android app checks each day on the tester's phone whether your app was opened, and reminds them in the evening if it wasn't.
  • A progress view for you: you see who is on day 6 and who stopped, so you know whom to nudge before the clock resets.
  • Ship an update in week two: it gives everyone a new reason to open the app and shows Google an active test.

When you don't have 12 friends

Most developers have three to eight people they can ask. You don't need to find the rest among strangers on forums.

  • Free test exchange: on Tester Exchange your phone gets other developers' apps to test automatically, and every app you test brings a tester to yours.
  • Skip testing: credits cover the seats your friends leave open, and paid apps get testers first. Members earn credits by testing, or you can buy a small pack.
  • Same rules for everyone: every tester, friend or not, is a real person on their own phone, and every day is verified the same way.

Frequently asked questions

Do friends and family count as testers for Google Play?

Yes, as long as each one uses their own Google account on their own Android phone, installs from your closed-testing opt-in link and stays opted in for the full 14 days.

Can I use my own second account or my partner's phone with several accounts?

Don't. Several accounts on one device, or accounts you made yourself, look like fake testers. One person, one account, one phone.

How often do friends need to open the app?

Google doesn't publish a number, but regular use across the whole test is what it looks for. Ask for a short session every day so that a few missed days don't matter.

What if only five friends can help?

Use them, then fill the other seats from the exchange. On Tester Exchange friends join free through your share link, and the exchange fills the rest.

Does a test with friends guarantee production access?

No. Google decides. Real, active testers and honest answers in the production-access questions give you the best chance.
