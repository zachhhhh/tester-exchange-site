The rule: 12 testers × 14 days

Google Play requires new personal developer accounts to run a closed test before applying for production access. The requirement:

  • At least 12 testers opted in to your closed testing track
  • They stay opted in for at least 14 continuous days
  • After that, you apply for production in Play Console and answer questions about your test
  • Organization accounts (with a D-U-N-S number) are exempt

The number was 20 testers until December 2024, when Google lowered it to 12. The 14-day clock only counts days with 12 or more testers opted in, so if testers leave and you drop to 11, you lose progress.

Why apps get rejected after 14 days

Many developers reach 14 days and still get rejected with a message about insufficient testing or engagement. Google looks at whether testers really opened and used the app, not only whether they opted in.

Installs from people who never open the app, or one shared phone with many accounts, don't count as real testing. What helps: testers who open the app regularly during the 14 days, feedback you can describe, and new builds that show you acted on it.

Set up the closed test in Play Console

  1. Open Play Console → your app → Test and release → Testing → Closed testing, and create a track (or use the default Alpha track).
  2. Under Testers, choose a Google Group or an email list. A Google Group is easiest: anyone who joins the group can opt in.
  3. Upload an app bundle to the track, set the countries, and send the release for review.
  4. Once it's approved, copy the opt-in link (https://play.google.com/apps/testing/your.package.name). Testers open it, tap Become a tester, then install from Google Play.
  5. Check the Testers tab in Play Console to see how many people have opted in.

Where to find 12 real testers

  • Friends and family. Free, but most people don't have 12 Android users willing to open an app daily for two weeks.
  • Test-for-test communities. Developers test each other's apps. Free, and testers understand what's needed. The risk is people who opt in and never open your app.
  • Paid tester services. Usually US$10–35 per app. Fast, but you're trusting that the testers are real and active.
  • Tester Exchange. A free test-for-test exchange where daily use is verified by a companion app on each tester's phone, and testers are paid in credits only for verified days.

How Tester Exchange works

  1. Add your app: paste its Google Play link at /start.
  2. Choose how to get testers: the free swap circle (6 developers test each other's apps, no credits), testers from the pool (start right away for 180 credits), or both.
  3. Install the Tester Exchange Android app on your phone and allow Usage access. It only checks the apps you joined.
  4. Add your testers to your closed test in Play Console.
  5. Testers open your app every day for 14 days, and the companion app on their phones confirms each day.
  6. After 14 days, open your report: daily active testers, feedback, and draft answers for Play Console's production-access questions.

Keep testers engaged for 14 days

  • Recruit 14–15 testers, not exactly 12, so a few dropouts don't reset your clock.
  • Tell testers what to try: one or two concrete tasks, not "please test my app".
  • Ship at least one update during the test, and fix what testers report.
  • Ask for written feedback. You'll need to summarize it when you apply for production.

Apply for production access

After 14 days, go to Play Console → Dashboard → Apply for production. Google asks about how you recruited testers, how they engaged with your app, the feedback you received, what you changed, and why the app is ready. Answer with real numbers: how many testers, how many days they used the app, what they reported, and which fixes you shipped. Reviews usually take up to 7 days.

Frequently asked questions

Do the 12 testers need to use the app every day?

Google requires them to stay opted in for 14 days and reviews engagement. Regular real use is what avoids an "insufficient testing" rejection.

Can I use my own phones or accounts as testers?

Google can tell when many accounts share one device, and it doesn't count as real testing. Use real people on their own phones.

Does a tester who leaves reset the 14 days?

The count only advances on days with at least 12 opted-in testers. Recruit a couple of extra testers so dropouts don't stall you.

Is Tester Exchange free?

Yes. The swap circle is free and uses no credits. Testers from the pool cost credits, which you earn by testing other apps (1 credit per verified day), and new members get free starting credits. Buying credits is optional.
