Organization vs personal Google Play developer account

Updated November 2026

The 12 testers, 14 days rule applies to personal developer accounts created after 13 November 2023. Organization accounts are not covered by it. That makes many solo developers ask whether they should register as a business instead. Sometimes that's the right call, but an organization account needs a real legal entity, a D-U-N-S number and a verified website. This guide compares both, using Google's Help Center as the source.

The two account types

  • Personal: Google describes it as meant for personal use, such as students, hobbyists and amateur developers. You verify your own legal name and address.
  • Organization: for businesses and organizations, including commercial, professional and government activity. You verify the organization with a D-U-N-S number, an official phone number and an organization website.

Google says both account types have the same core functionality. Both can sell apps and in-app products once a payments profile is set up.

The testing difference

Google requires personal accounts created after 13 November 2023 to run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for at least 14 days, then apply for production access. Organization accounts don't have this step: they can release to production once the app passes normal review.

That saves about three weeks per app. But it is the only real advantage for most indie developers, and getting an organization account can take longer than three weeks if you don't already have a company and a D-U-N-S number.

When an organization account is required

Google says some kinds of apps must be published from an organization account. These include:

  • Financial products and services, such as banking, loans, stock trading and crypto wallets or exchanges.
  • Health apps, such as medical apps and human subjects research apps.
  • Apps approved to use Android's VpnService class.
  • Government apps, made by or for a government agency.

If your app is in one of these categories, you need an organization account regardless of testing.

What an organization account needs

  • A D-U-N-S number for your legal entity from Dun & Bradstreet. Google says you can't create an organization account without a valid one. Applying is free; Google warns about third-party services that charge high fees for it. It can take days to weeks to arrive.
  • A legal entity whose name and address match the D-U-N-S record and your Google payments profile.
  • An organization website that you verify in Play Console.
  • An official organization phone number, plus contact and public developer email addresses and phone numbers, each verified with a one-time code.
  • The one-time registration fee that every new developer account pays.

Converting a personal account

Google's Help Center describes a way to change a personal account into an organization account. In outline:

  1. Make sure your personal account is fully verified, with no pending warnings on the Play Console home page.
  2. Go to Manage account, then Account details, then About you, and verify your organization's website.
  3. Click Change account type and choose or create an organization payments profile with your D-U-N-S number.
  4. Enter organization details and contacts, and verify each email and phone.
  5. Complete any identity verification Google asks for, then link the payments profile and save.

Google says you can't change an organization account back to personal, and that you should wait at least 72 hours after switching before submitting new apps. Google's page on converting doesn't spell out how apps already in closed testing are treated, so check your Dashboard after the switch before you stop a running test.

Trade-offs

  • Cost: forming a company can mean registration fees, an accountant and yearly filings, depending on your country. Twelve testers cost far less.
  • Time: D-U-N-S plus website verification plus organization checks can take longer than a 14-day test.
  • Credibility: a company name can look more trustworthy to users and partners.
  • No way back: you can't convert an organization account to personal.

Which should you choose?

  1. Your app is finance, health, VPN or government: organization account.
  2. You already have a company with a D-U-N-S number: organization account. It skips the test.
  3. You're a solo developer or hobbyist without a company: stay personal and run the closed test. It's faster and cheaper than forming a company only to skip it.
  4. You plan many apps and a business anyway: start the company and D-U-N-S process, and run closed tests on your personal account while you wait.

Frequently asked questions

Do organization accounts need 12 testers?

No. The closed testing requirement applies to personal accounts created after 13 November 2023.

Can I get a D-U-N-S number as an individual?

D-U-N-S numbers are issued to business entities. Sole proprietorships can be registered in some countries; check with Dun & Bradstreet for yours. Applying is free.

Can I switch from personal to organization?

Yes. Google describes a Change account type option under Manage account, Account details. It needs a verified organization website and a D-U-N-S number.

Can I switch back to personal later?

No. Google says organization accounts can't be changed to personal. You would need a new account and an app transfer.

Is an organization account worth it just to skip testing?

Usually not for a solo developer. Forming a company and getting verified often costs more time and money than a 14-day closed test.
