Rejected after closed testing? How to fix "more testing required"

Updated October 2026

You ran a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days, applied for production, and Google said your app needs more testing. This is the most common outcome for new personal accounts. It usually means Google didn't see enough real use of your app during the test. Here's what Google looks at, and how to run a second test that passes.

Why Google says your app needs more testing

Opting in is not the same as testing. Google can see how many testers installed the app, how often they opened it, and whether you shipped updates during the test. A test where people installed once and never came back looks like a box being ticked, not a real test.

  • Low engagement: testers installed the app but rarely or never opened it.
  • Too few active testers: people left the test, and you dropped below 12 opted-in testers.
  • No iteration: no new builds and no visible response to feedback during the 14 days.
  • Weak answers: vague replies in the production questionnaire, with no numbers and no examples of feedback.

A plan that passes on the second try

  1. Keep the closed test running. Don't delete the track or remove testers who are still active.
  2. Recruit 14–15 real testers who will open the app daily, so a few dropouts don't push you under 12.
  3. Give testers one or two concrete tasks per week ("create an account, add an item, change a setting").
  4. Ask for written feedback, fix at least a few reported issues, and upload new builds to the closed track during the test.
  5. Keep a log: daily active testers, total tester-days, feedback received, and what you changed.
  6. After 14 more days of real use, apply again and answer every question with those numbers.

Show proof of engagement

Your answers should read like a short test report: how many testers, how many days they used the app, what they reported, and which builds fixed it. Tester Exchange records each day every tester opens your app and drafts these answers from that data, so you don't have to reconstruct them from memory.

What not to do

  • Don't use your own spare phones or accounts as testers. Many accounts on one device don't count as real testing.
  • Don't pay for "testers" who only install and never open the app. That's exactly what gets rejected.
  • Don't copy generic answers from the internet. Use your own numbers and your own feedback.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to restart the 14 days?

You keep your closed test, but Google wants to see more real testing before you apply again. Plan for at least another 14 days of active use.

How often should testers open the app?

Google doesn't publish a number. Daily use by most testers is the safest pattern, and it's what Tester Exchange records for every tester.

Does feedback matter?

Yes. The questionnaire asks what testers said and what you changed. Real feedback and real fixes make your answers credible.
